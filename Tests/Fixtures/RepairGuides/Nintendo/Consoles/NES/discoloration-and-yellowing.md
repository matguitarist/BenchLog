# Discoloration and Yellowing

## Problem Description
Many NES consoles develop a yellow or brown discoloration over time. This is especially common with the light gray plastic used in the original NES case. This yellowing is not dirt or grime, but a chemical change in the plastic itself caused by exposure to ultraviolet light, heat, and environmental factors. This issue affects the appearance but not the functionality of the console.

![[yellowed_nes.jpg]]

## Scientific Explanation
The yellowing is caused by bromine, which was added to plastics as a flame retardant. When exposed to UV light and oxygen over time, the bromine compounds break down and cause the characteristic yellow color. This process is known as photo-oxidation and affects many electronics from the 1980s and 1990s.

![[yellowing_comparison.jpg]]

## Common Characteristics
- Yellowing is typically uneven, often worse on the top case
- Some consoles yellow more severely than others
- The discoloration may continue to worsen over time
- Different batches of NES consoles may yellow at different rates
- Controllers and accessories can also be affected

## Required Materials for Restoration
- Hydrogen peroxide (3% to 12%)
- UV light source (sunlight or UV lamps)
- Clear plastic container large enough for NES parts
- Protective gloves
- Safety goggles
- Plastic wrap or transparent lid
- Cream developer (40 volume) from beauty supply stores (optional)
- OxiClean (optional)
- Soft cloths
- Fine-grit sandpaper (1500-2000 grit, optional)
- Plastic polish (optional)

## Safety Precautions
- Always wear gloves when handling hydrogen peroxide
- Work in a well-ventilated area
- Wear eye protection
- Keep chemicals away from children and pets
- Do not mix hydrogen peroxide with ammonia or vinegar
- Thoroughly rinse all parts after treatment

## Restoration Methods

### Method 1: The Sunlight Method (Basic)
1. Disassemble the NES by removing the six screws on the bottom
2. Separate the case halves and remove all internal components
3. Clean the plastic thoroughly with mild soap and water
4. In a clear container, submerge the yellowed plastic in 3% hydrogen peroxide
5. Cover the container with plastic wrap or a transparent lid
6. Place in direct sunlight for 8-12 hours
7. Check progress every hour and flip the parts for even exposure
8. Rinse thoroughly with water when desired whiteness is achieved
9. Dry completely before reassembly

![[sunlight_method.jpg]]

### Method 2: The "Retr0bright" Method (Advanced)
This method uses a homemade mixture for better results:
1. Prepare the mixture:
   - 1 part 40 volume cream developer
   - 1-2 tablespoons of OxiClean (without chlorine)
   - Optional: few drops of dish soap as a wetting agent
2. Mix to create a gel-like consistency
3. Apply evenly to yellowed surfaces
4. Wrap in plastic wrap to prevent drying out
5. Place under UV light or in direct sunlight
6. Check progress every 30-60 minutes
7. Process may take 4-8 hours depending on severity
8. Rinse thoroughly with water
9. Repeat if necessary for severe yellowing

![[retrobright_method.jpg]]

### Method 3: UV Light Box Method (Most Consistent)
For more controlled results:
1. Build or purchase a UV light box
2. Place parts in the box submerged in hydrogen peroxide
3. Turn on UV lights and close the box
4. Maintain temperature between 75-85°F for best results
5. Check progress every hour
6. Treat for 6-12 hours as needed
7. Rinse and dry thoroughly

![[uv_lightbox.jpg]]

## Important Considerations

### Potential Risks
1. **Over-bleaching**: Can cause whitening beyond original color or make plastic brittle
2. **Uneven results**: Some areas may whiten more than others
3. **Text/labels**: Printed text on the plastic may fade or disappear
4. **Temporary effect**: Yellowing may return over time
5. **Plastic damage**: Excessive treatment can weaken the plastic

### Re-yellowing Prevention
After restoration:
1. Store the NES away from direct sunlight
2. Keep in a cool, dry environment
3. Consider applying UV-resistant clear coat (controversial as it may yellow itself)
4. Use a dust cover when not in use
5. Maintain consistent temperature (avoid hot environments)

![[restored_comparison.jpg]]

## Restoration of NES Controllers and Accessories
The same methods work for controllers and accessories, but with these considerations:
1. Remove all electronic components first
2. Take photos before disassembly to aid reassembly
3. Pay special attention to buttons and D-pads
4. Text and logos on controllers may be more susceptible to fading
5. Small parts may need shorter treatment times

## Alternative Solutions

### Painting
For severely yellowed consoles that don't respond to bleaching:
1. Sand the surface lightly
2. Apply plastic primer
3. Use matching spray paint designed for plastics
4. Apply clear coat for protection

### Replacement Cases
For collectors seeking perfect appearance:
1. Donor consoles with better condition cases
2. Third-party reproduction cases (limited availability)
3. 3D printed replacements (emerging option)

![[case_alternatives.jpg]]

## Expert Tips
1. Yellowed plastic often whitens beyond what seems possible - be patient
2. Multiple shorter sessions often work better than one long session
3. Stirring the hydrogen peroxide occasionally improves results
4. Adding a small aquarium bubbler to the container can speed up the process
5. Light polishing after treatment can restore shine
6. Different plastic formulations respond differently - results may vary
