# NES Model Identification

## Why Model Identification Matters
The Nintendo Entertainment System (NES) went through several revisions during its production run from 1985 to 1995. Identifying which model you have is important for:
- Determining repair procedures
- Finding compatible replacement parts
- Understanding common issues specific to your model
- Authenticating collectible systems
- Appropriate maintenance and restoration

![[nes_models_overview.jpg]]

## Main NES Models

### Front-Loading NES (NES-001)
The original and most common model in North America and Europe:
- Released in 1985 in North America (1986-1987 in Europe)
- Front-loading cartridge mechanism ("toaster" design)
- Light gray and dark gray plastic casing
- Available as both "Deluxe Set" and "Control Deck" bundles

#### Identifying Features:
- "Nintendo Entertainment System" text on front
- Front-loading cartridge slot with spring-loaded door
- Two controller ports on front left side
- Power and Reset buttons on right side
- Expansion port on bottom
- RF switch box for TV connection (early models)
- RF and A/V outputs on back (later models)

![[front_loader_nes.jpg]]

### Top-Loading NES (NES-101)
The redesigned model released later in the NES lifecycle:
- Released in 1993
- Top-loading cartridge slot (similar to Super NES)
- Smaller footprint than original NES
- Light gray color (slightly different shade than original)
- Budget-focused redesign

#### Identifying Features:
- "Nintendo Entertainment System" text on front
- Top-loading cartridge slot (no door)
- Two controller ports on front
- "Power" and "Reset" buttons on left side
- No expansion port on bottom
- Dog-bone shaped controllers (rounded design)
- RF output only (no A/V output)

![[top_loader_nes.jpg]]

## Front-Loading NES Revisions
The original NES-001 went through several internal revisions while maintaining the same external appearance:

### Motherboard Revisions

#### "NROM" Motherboards (NES-CPU-01 through NES-CPU-06)
Early revisions (1985-1988):
- Larger RF shielding
- 5-screw RF shield design
- Original video and audio hardware
- CIC lockout chip in early revision

#### "NROM-02" Motherboards (NES-CPU-07 through NES-CPU-09)
Mid-life revisions (1988-1990):
- Consolidated chips
- Fewer components
- 3-screw RF shield design
- Minor circuit improvements

#### "NROM-04" Motherboards (NES-CPU-10 through NES-CPU-11)
Late revisions (1990-1993):
- Further consolidated chips
- Improved power regulation
- Cost-reduction measures
- Compatible with later games

## How to Identify Your Specific NES Model

### Method 1: Serial Number
Look at the bottom of your NES for a sticker with manufacturing information:
- Serial numbers typically begin with "NES-001"
- The next set of numbers indicates manufacturing date
- Format may be year followed by week (e.g., "8937" = 37th week of 1989)

![[serial_number_location.jpg]]

### Method 2: Motherboard Inspection
Opening your NES to check the motherboard:
1. Remove the six screws from the bottom
2. Lift off the top case
3. Remove the RF shield
4. Look for motherboard identification:
   - Printed model number (e.g., "NES-CPU-07")
   - Manufacturing date stamps
   - Chip arrangements unique to specific revisions

![[motherboard_identification.jpg]]

### Method 3: External Features
Some external differences can help identify revisions:
1. **A/V Output**:
   - Early models: RF output only
   - Later models: Both RF and composite A/V outputs
2. **Controller Port Design**:
   - Subtle changes in the molding of controller ports
3. **Nintendo Seal of Quality**:
   - Early models: Round seal
   - Later models: Oval seal

## Regional Variants

### North American NES (NTSC)
- Light gray and dark gray color scheme
- Larger cartridges with end labels
- Runs at 60Hz

### European NES (PAL)
- Similar appearance to North American version
- Cartridges often have different labels
- Runs at 50Hz
- Different power supply (220-240V vs. 110-120V)

### Asian/Australian NES (PAL or NTSC-J)
- Similar to North American or European depending on region
- May have different lockout chip implementation
- Power supply variations

![[regional_variants.jpg]]

## Japanese Famicom (Not NES)
The Japanese version differs significantly from international NES models:
- Released in 1983 (before NES)
- Red and white color scheme
- Top-loading cartridge slot
- Controllers hardwired to console
- Smaller cartridges
- Different expansion options

![[famicom_system.jpg]]

## Special Editions and Variants

### NES Action Set
- Bundle with console, two controllers, Zapper, and Super Mario Bros./Duck Hunt

### NES Power Set
- Bundle with console, two controllers, Power Pad, and Super Mario Bros./Duck Hunt/World Class Track Meet

### NES Control Deck
- Basic bundle with console, two controllers, and Super Mario Bros.

### Test Market NES (Rare)
- Limited release in New York and Los Angeles in 1985
- Similar to Japanese Famicom layout but in NES colors
- Square buttons on controllers
- Extremely rare collector's item

![[test_market_nes.jpg]]

## Repair Considerations by Model

### Front-Loader Specific Issues
- 72-pin connector problems common to all revisions
- Blinking red light issue affects all models
- Later revisions may have more reliable power regulation
- Earlier models more prone to video output issues

### Top-Loader Specific Issues
- More reliable design overall (no 72-pin connector issues)
- RF-only output (lower quality video)
- Known for video "jailbar" interference
- Different cartridge slot requires different cleaning methods

## Identifying Reproduction and Counterfeit Systems
Be aware of non-authentic systems:
- Check for proper Nintendo branding and trademark symbols
- Authentic Nintendo logos should be molded into the plastic
- Weight difference (reproductions often lighter)
- Build quality differences in plastic texture and color
- Screws and mounting points often differ from originals
